## URD-032 — The path's stage-open scroll is a bigger jump than it needs to be, and can go stale mid-session
attempts: 0
files: src/screens/HomeScreen.tsx
definition of done: Two related, non-blocking gaps in URD-002's accordion
  fix, both found in round-2 critique of commit `1344973`:
  (1) Opening a course stage resets scroll to the very top of the page
  (past the greeting, level card, continue card and today's-word card)
  rather than to the accordion itself. DESIGN CRITIC measured this costs
  a real but short scroll back down (~500px, not the ~8000px round trip
  the bug being fixed would have cost) — not disorienting, just not as
  tight as it could be. A middle ground (landing just above the
  accordion, skipping the fixed cards above it) would still guarantee a
  labeled, non-empty state — the collapsed stage list itself is fully
  labeled — while roughly halving the return scroll.
  (2) The mount-time auto-scroll-to-current-lesson (`didAutoScroll` ref,
  fires once per mount) does not re-fire when `currentLevel` advances
  mid-session (e.g. a learner finishes their last lesson in a stage while
  scrolled elsewhere). The accordion itself correctly re-opens the new
  current stage now (URD-002's round-2 fix), but nothing scrolls to reveal
  it if the learner isn't already looking at that part of the screen.
verify: a script driving the real built app confirms (a) opening a stage
  from a scrolled-down position lands within some tighter bound (e.g.
  under 1000px of scroll to reach the newly-open content) than the current
  full-reset, and (b) completing a stage's last lesson mid-session scrolls
  the now-current stage into view without requiring a manual scroll.
notes: Both found and measured (not just theorized) reviewing URD-002's
  round-2 fix — DESIGN CRITIC measured the ~500px vs ~8000px distinction
  directly; THE CRITIC found (2) while checking for effect-ordering risk
  between the accordion's new `currentLevel`-reset effect and the
  pre-existing mount-time auto-scroll. Neither blocked URD-002: the
  current behavior is always labeled and never traps a learner, matching
  the bar both critics graded against. Two precise-positioning approaches
  were already tried and measured wrong against the real react-native-web
  build while fixing URD-002 (documented in HomeScreen.tsx) — whoever
  picks this up should read that history before trying `measureLayout` or
  tracked-`onScroll`-offset again.

  RESOLVED (a): added a `fixedHeaderHeight` ref, captured once via
  `onLayout` on a wrapper `View` around everything above the accordion
  (greeting, level card, continue card, today's-word card, jump-ahead
  hint). `toggleLevel`'s scroll-to-open now targets
  `fixedHeaderHeight.current` instead of `0`. This sidesteps both of the
  previously-failed precise-positioning attempts: no measurement is taken
  AT TOGGLE TIME (the fixed region's height doesn't change when an
  accordion stage collapses or expands, so a once-captured value can't go
  stale the way `measureLayout`'s live query and the tracked-`onScroll`
  offset both did against the real build).

  RESOLVED (b): the existing `useEffect(() => { setOpenLevel(undefined);
  }, [currentLevel])` also sets `didAutoScroll.current = false` inside its
  body, re-arming the one-shot auto-scroll guard specifically when
  `currentLevel` legitimately advances.

  Added `scripts/check-home-scroll.js` (`check:home-scroll`) for part (a):
  drives the real built bundle, seeds guest progress ~70% through the
  course (matching `check-path.js`'s own scenario, so several stages sit
  collapsed below the open one), scrolls to the bottom, opens a collapsed
  stage, and asserts scroll lands above 0px (not the pre-URD-032 full
  reset) and under 1500px (a tight landing, not a near-full ~8,000px
  return scroll), with the newly-opened stage's own header visible near
  the top. Confirmed deterministic across repeat runs (539px landing every
  time) and confirmed to fail correctly under induced failure (reverting
  the scroll target to `y: 0` reproduces "reset scroll to the very top").

  THE CRITIC (mandatory) and DESIGN CRITIC (this changes a screen, and
  DESIGN CRITIC's own measurement is cited in this item's history) were
  dispatched against the (a)-only, (b)-unverified state above.

  DESIGN CRITIC: no BLOCKING authority. Drove the real app, screenshotted
  the landing (clean — the newly-opened stage's own header is
  unambiguously the visual anchor, no blank space, no clipped content, no
  side effect from the new `collapsable={false}` wrapper View, tap
  targets unaffected: 372×94 to 372×130 across all four stage headers).
  Found one real MAJOR: frame-by-frame tracing of the "animated"
  scroll-to-open showed ~99% of the ~9,035px travel happened as an
  instantaneous, unanimated jump within a single frame gap (≤62ms) — the
  browser's own scrollTop clamp firing synchronously on the same-tick
  reflow from the previously-open stage collapsing — with only the
  final ~349px actually easing in over ~320ms. Net effect: a hard
  teleport followed by a small corrective wobble, not one continuous
  "return to context" motion. FIXED: deferred the `scrollTo` call out of
  `toggleLevel`'s own tick and into a `useEffect` keyed on `openLevel`,
  firing on the next animation frame after the collapse has already
  committed and the clamp has already happened invisibly — the
  subsequent `scrollTo` then glides smoothly from wherever the clamp
  actually landed to `fixedHeaderHeight.current`, one motion instead of
  two. Re-verified via `check:home-scroll` (still 539px, deterministic)
  after the change; the frame-level re-trace was not repeated (would need
  redispatching DESIGN CRITIC), but the mechanism directly addresses the
  reported defect (removing the synchronous same-tick coupling between
  the collapse and the scroll call is what caused the clamp-then-glide
  split in the first place).

  THE CRITIC: found one BLOCKING issue, no MAJOR beyond it, two MINOR.
  BLOCKING: the re-arm at (b) never actually fires the auto-scroll on the
  transition it exists for. The reset effect (keyed on `currentLevel`)
  was declared *after* the auto-scroll effect (keyed on `currentId`) in
  the component; a real stage advance changes both in the same commit,
  and React runs a component's effects in hook-declaration order within
  one commit — so the auto-scroll effect always ran first, read the
  guard as still `true` from its earlier mount-time fire, and bailed
  before the reset (declared later) ever landed. Reproduced in an
  isolated react/react-dom 18.3.1 + jsdom harness mirroring the two
  effects' declared order. FIXED: moved the `openLevel` state and the
  reset effect to before the auto-scroll effect in the component body, so
  the reset lands in the same commit but earlier in hook order — the
  auto-scroll effect now always sees the just-reset guard, never the
  stale one. MINOR: `fixedHeaderHeight.current` has no floor/fallback if
  `onLayout` hasn't fired yet (would silently reproduce the old
  full-reset bug in that narrow, likely-unreachable window — not
  defended against, not tested either way). MINOR:
  `check:home-scroll` exercises only the open-from-bottom scenario, not
  opening from near the top or the collapse path (verified by reading
  that `toggleLevel` only scrolls `if (opening)`, not by a check).
  Neither MINOR was actioned — narrow, and not required to un-block.

  A live, working end-to-end reproduction of part (b)'s exact scenario
  was then built (`scripts/check-stage-advance-scroll.js`, formalized
  and committed): seed guest progress so a single beginner lesson
  remains, scroll away from the mount-time auto-scroll's landing spot,
  complete that lesson for real — walking it via
  `check-stability.js`'s own proven answer/advance pattern, since
  correctness doesn't matter and `finishLesson` records completion
  regardless of accuracy — and confirm the accordion opens the next
  stage, scroll actually moved, and the new current lesson is visible.
  No page reload anywhere (a reload would remount `HomeScreen` and defeat
  the whole test — the bug only exists because it does not remount
  between lessons).

  This measured PASS against the fixed build (619px landing,
  deterministic across repeat runs). Per this project's own "break a
  check on purpose and watch it fail" standard, the fix was then
  induced-failure tested: the two effects were temporarily swapped back
  to their original (buggy) declared order, rebuilt, and re-run — and
  the check **still passed**. Repeated, then re-run against a build
  instrumented to log every render's `currentId`/`currentLevel` pair and
  whether the auto-scroll guard read true or false at each of its own
  runs: the render log confirmed `currentId` and `currentLevel` always
  change together in a single render (never split across two commits, as
  the theory assumed), but the auto-scroll effect's guard read `false` at
  the point that mattered regardless of which effect was declared first.
  THE CRITIC's mechanism — reproduced cleanly in an isolated harness — does
  not reproduce against the real component, for a reason not fully
  root-caused this session (a candidate but unconfirmed explanation: the
  isolated harness may not have matched some detail of how React 18's
  automatic batching or Zustand's `set()` calls inside `finishLesson`
  actually sequence renders in the live app). The reorder fix ships
  anyway — it is strictly safer regardless of mechanism (resetting a
  guard before its own reader can only help, never hurt) and matches the
  documented intent — and `check:stage-advance-scroll` is committed
  permanently to hold the *observed* behavior in place rather than assert
  a specific internal mechanism, the same principle every other check
  script in this project already follows.

  This is reported plainly rather than smoothed into "verified, passing"
  — a check that could not be made to fail under the specific fault it
  was written to catch is a different, weaker result than one that
  failed and was then fixed, and this project's own standard (item 2 of
  `CLAUDE.md`'s non-negotiables) says exactly that "a check that has
  never failed is a hypothesis." This one failed to fail; that is worth
  knowing, not hiding.
