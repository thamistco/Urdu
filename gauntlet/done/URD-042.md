## URD-042 — Half the alphabet gets no review exposure across the back two-thirds of the course
attempts: 0
files: src/lib/review.ts, src/exercises/generator.ts
definition of done: with only one letter slot per review from roughly u14
  on (URD-017), and `reviewLetterPool`'s per-review pool shuffled with a
  seed keyed on `lessonId` alone, each review effectively draws one letter
  from an independent shuffle of the full 46-letter course-wide pool.
  Measured directly: tallying every letter that appears in a
  `letterForm`/`letterPick`/`letterTrace` exercise across all reviews u10
  through u39 (30 reviews) with nothing due, only 21 of 40 letters (52.5%)
  are ever touched; 19 never appear, including `be`/`pe` — the very first
  letter pair taught, in `l-1` — and three of the four Urdu "z"-sound
  letters (`zaal`, `ze`, `zhe`). Since the shuffle is seeded (deterministic,
  not per-play), this is the fixed, reproducible content of the course
  today, not sampling noise that might average out. Give letter selection
  across reviews some notion of coverage — round-robin, a stored
  last-reviewed-letter cursor, or similar — so a learner who studies the
  whole course actually meets every letter again somewhere in it, not just
  the ones an independent per-review shuffle happened to surface first.
verify: a test simulating every review lesson in course order and asserting
  every letter in `LETTERS` is drawn by at least one of them.
notes: Found by CURRICULUM CRITIC reviewing URD-017. A structural
  consequence of two independently-reasonable pre-existing designs
  (per-review independent shuffle; one letter slot per late review) meeting
  for the first time once URD-017 made "one letter slot" the norm rather
  than "four or five." Not blocking, and not something URD-017 itself
  promised to fix — its own acceptance bar ("reaching near zero") is what
  makes one slot the norm in the first place — but a real coverage gap
  worth its own item, since fixing it well means changing how letters are
  *selected* across reviews, not just how many are asked per review.

  RESOLVED, across two critic rounds, both with real findings.

  Two-part fix in `src/lib/review.ts`. First, `rotatingCoverageOrder`
  rotates ONE fixed shuffle of a candidate set (keyed on a constant, not
  per-lesson) by a combined `reviewIndex + visit` offset, instead of
  reshuffling independently per review — `prioritizedLetterPool` is the
  letter-side equivalent of the pre-existing `prioritizedPool` using this
  rotation. Measured: rotation alone still missed 6 of 40 letters, because
  the candidate set itself (course-wide taught letters) *grows* across the
  first several reviews rather than staying fixed, so an early review's
  offset can land on a not-yet-taught letter and waste its one shot at
  ever being "first" for it.

  Second, `computeLetterCoverageAssignment` (internal, memoized) closes
  that gap directly: walking every real review lesson in path order once,
  treating a single fixed shuffle of all 40 letters as a claim queue, each
  review claims the earliest still-unclaimed, already-taught letter. Since
  total letter-question capacity across all reviews (58, measured) exceeds
  the alphabet (40), every letter is guaranteed a claiming review before
  the course ends. `reviewLetterPool` forces the assigned letter to the
  front of its pool only when it's already a legitimate candidate for that
  specific call — never overriding the existing "never surface ungraded
  material" guarantee.

  `reviewIndex` (which review lesson, in path order) is computed once in
  `generator.ts`'s review branch, shared with URD-041's own letter-*kind*
  offset (no longer duplicated), and threaded through both
  `fallbackReviewRefs` call sites.

  Verified directly: touched letters across all 41 real reviews went from
  30/40 (already improved incidentally by URD-039/041's own visit/
  reviewIndex threading) to 40/40, on both the `both` and `script` tracks.

  **Round 1 critic findings, both fixed:**

  CURRICULUM CRITIC — MAJOR: the assigned coverage letter was forced to
  the front of the pool unconditionally on *every* visit. With
  `letterCount === 1` (the norm from about u14 on, URD-017),
  `fallbackReviewRefs` takes exactly `pool[0]`, so every one of 40 reviews
  with an assignment froze to one single letter forever, for every replay,
  for every learner — silently reintroducing URD-039's own repeat-visit-
  staleness bug on the letter axis. Reproduced live: `rev-together` drew
  `daal` and only `daal` across 6 sampled visits. Fixed: the force only
  applies on `visit === 0` — a learner playing the course straight through
  still meets every letter once (the whole-course guarantee this item asks
  for), while every visit after that returns to the normal rotation URD-039
  already established, undoing none of it.

  THE CRITIC — a different MAJOR: two new tests meant to pin the
  known-restriction guard (never surface a letter the learner hasn't been
  graded on, even while pursuing coverage) both hardcoded
  `rev-the-wider-world` — which turns out to be the *one* review lesson
  (of 41) with no coverage assignment at all, so the guard they claimed to
  test was never actually exercised. Proved this concretely by injecting
  the exact bug (removing the `pool.includes(assigned)` check) and
  watching both tests still pass unchanged. Fixed: both retargeted to
  sweep every real review instead of one hardcoded lesson; re-injecting
  the same bug now correctly fails both, naming `rev-first-faces` (a
  review that does carry an assignment).

  **Round 2** (THE CRITIC re-dispatched specifically to verify round 1's
  two fixes): confirmed both genuinely resolved by direct re-execution —
  `rev-together`/`rev-the-modern-world` vary across visits 0-5 while the
  visit=0 full-course sweep still measures 40/40; the retargeted tests,
  with the bug reintroduced, fail and correctly name an assigned review.
  Found one new MINOR: `generator.test.ts`'s retargeted test lacked the
  empty-taught-words guard `review.test.ts`'s sibling test already carries
  for the identical edge case (inert against today's real content — no
  review currently has zero taught words at its position — but a latent
  false-failure risk against a future content change). Fixed: added the
  identical guard for symmetry.

  CURRICULUM CRITIC's own remaining observations, recorded but not acted
  on (real, not blocking, outside this item's own definition of done):
  the deterministic once-per-course assignment has no curricular logic
  behind *which* review reinforces *which* letter, a pure fixed-shuffle
  artifact — defensible for reinforcement content rather than new
  instruction, but worth naming; two of the latest-taught letters
  (`do-chashmi-he`, `noon-ghunna`) land their guarantee in the final 2 of
  41 reviews, under-protecting exactly the hardest-taught letters against
  ordinary learner attrition; the 40/40 guarantee is a clean-room
  ("nothing due") measurement, weaker in practice once real due-queue
  content competes for the same filler slots. THE CRITIC's own
  informational note: the claim-queue algorithm has exactly one review of
  slack given today's course shape (32 unclaimed letters, 32 fully-
  eligible reviews) — tight but not broken, and the sweep test that
  already exists would catch a future regression there directly.
