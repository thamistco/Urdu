## URD-043 — A letter's last sighting in its lesson is usually the easy kind, not the hard one
attempts: 0
files: src/exercises/generator.ts
definition of done: URD-019 grades an item's SRS state on the last of its
  sightings this lesson visit (with a same/last-two-sightings-agree guard).
  That is provably the hardest, most diagnostic demand for vocabulary — every
  one of the 2,281 words' staggered climb ends on `produce` (`typeWord` or
  `wordBuild`), 100% of the time, both tracks — but not for letters. Measured
  directly across all 9 letter lessons: `letterExerciseAt`'s turn rotation
  (`t = (round + idx + turnOffset) % 3`, cycling `letterTrace`(produce) →
  `letterForm`(recognise) → `letterPick`(recognise)) lands a letter's *final*
  round on a recognise-tier kind 67.4% of the time (31 of 46 letters
  sampled), because `idx`/`turnOffset` shift which phase falls last largely
  arbitrarily, unlike vocabulary's fixed ascending climb. Give the letter
  pipeline the same property vocabulary already has: its last sighting is
  reliably its hardest one.
verify: a test building every real letter lesson and asserting each letter's
  final sighting is `letterTrace` (produce), not `letterForm`/`letterPick`.
notes: Found by CURRICULUM CRITIC reviewing URD-019. Not blocking — nothing
  crashes, and URD-019's own last-two-sightings-agree guard (added during
  this same critique) already cuts the practical risk of a single lucky
  final guess from ~1-in-4 to ~1-in-16 regardless of which demand tier that
  guess happens to be on — but the deeper mismatch this finding names is
  real and separate: even a *confirmed* recognise-tier pair of sightings is
  weaker evidence of recall than a confirmed produce-tier pair would be, and
  today only vocabulary's pipeline is designed to guarantee the stronger one.

  RESOLVED, after two rejected intermediate designs (both caught by real
  measurement, not assumed clean) and two critic rounds with real findings.

  The `SIGHTINGS_PER_LETTER = 6` rounds now split: rounds 0 through
  `tailRound - 1` (4 rounds) stay round-major exactly as before, with
  ordinary 3-way kind variety. The last two rounds (`tailRound`,
  `finalRound`) are handled letter-major instead — each letter's own
  two-exercise tail pair is emitted back to back: `tailRound` restricted to
  `letterForm`/`letterPick` only (never `letterTrace`), `finalRound` always
  forced to `letterTrace`. Verified directly: 46 of 46 letters, on both
  `both` and `script` tracks, now genuinely end on `letterTrace`.

  **Two designs tried and rejected before this one, both by real
  measurement:**

  1. Forcing the final round's kind round-major (same round, same forced
     kind, for every letter) correctly made every letter's true last
     sighting `letterTrace`, but grouped every letter's forced-trace
     exercise into one contiguous block — round-major visits every letter
     within a round before moving on — producing a run of `letters.length`
     (4-7) identical `letterTrace` exercises in a row, the exact
     repetition problem this file's own history already rejected once for
     "round alone." `check:shape`'s 3-in-a-row ceiling (not gating
     `check:all`, but real) caught it: 9 of 9 real letter lessons exceeded
     it, worst `l-3` at 7 straight.
  2. Fixing that by pairing letter-major but leaving the *other* rounds'
     3-way cycling untouched instead pushed `letterTrace`'s overall share
     of a lesson too high — `check:shape`'s 40% single-kind ceiling caught
     5 lessons over it, worst `l-1` at 42%. `letterTrace` needed to move
     *where* it lands, not *how often* it's asked.

  The shipped design closes both: letter-major pairing avoids the run
  (consecutive letters' pairs alternate non-trace/trace/non-trace/trace,
  never two `letterTrace` adjacent), and restricting the non-final tail
  slot to recognise-tier-only keeps `letterTrace`'s total frequency at its
  original ~1/6 share.

  The same restructuring broke an existing **gating** unit test (URD-022's
  confusable-adjacency exact-count check for `l-3`, whose `re`-family is
  more than half its 7-letter group) and `check:shape`'s identical
  sub-check — both assumed the whole sequence stays uniform round-major
  throughout (`rounds = exercises.length / n`) to compute how many forced
  wrap-adjacencies to expect, and the letter-major tail broke that
  assumption (the true count dropped from 5 to 4). Fixed both with a
  generalized, structure-agnostic measure: count fresh (non-consecutive)
  occurrences of the sequence's own first letter to directly measure how
  many times a full pass through the group actually restarts, rather than
  assuming a shape — verified this reduces to the exact old count whenever
  the sequence really is uniform round-major (a generalization, not a
  special case for this one design).

  THE CRITIC and CURRICULUM CRITIC both dispatched (real generator code;
  this item's own discoverer for the curriculum half). THE CRITIC: no
  BLOCKING, no MAJOR — independently re-derived the core claim, the
  generalized formula's correctness, and confirmed `check:shape` and every
  relevant check script clean. Two MINOR, one fixed (a stale doc-comment
  cross-reference to a symbol that no longer exists) and one noted, not
  queued (a fallback branch for a letter with no context word is correct

  CURRICULUM CRITIC found two real MAJORs, both fixed via a documented
  lead overrule rather than further restructuring:

  1. A prior comment falsely justified the letter-major pairing by
     claiming it "mirrors how vocabulary already sequences recall→produce
     close together" — checked against the vocab lesson's own staggered-
     pass design and real generated timing, this is false: a vocabulary
     word's successive sightings land 3-7 exercises apart specifically
     because massed repetition inside ten seconds is not spacing (that
     design's own stated reason). Rewritten to state the real
     justification (structural necessity, not a vocab precedent) and to
     record why the adjacency itself doesn't cost what it would for
     vocabulary: `letterTrace` (`TraceExercise.tsx`) names the letter and
     position outright and asks the learner to draw it — a
     motor-formation check on an already-identified letter, not the
     blind-recall check vocabulary's spacing rule protects.
  2. `contextRound`'s range can coincide with the tail round — measured
     directly: exactly one letter per lesson, 9 of 9 real lessons, has its
     second-to-last sighting be the context word instead of a
     recognise-tier drill, undocumented by the prior comment. Verified
     concretely (`l-1`'s `te`/`w-dost` pairing: the context word shows
     `te` in a different position than the one traced immediately after)
     that this is a milder, not stronger, exposure than the recognise-tier
     alternative it occasionally replaces. Documented explicitly rather
     than coded around — narrowing `contextRound` further would only
     worsen the pigeonhole collision this file already accepts, for a risk
     measured to be lower than the alternative.
