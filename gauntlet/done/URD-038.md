## URD-038 — ذ ز ض ظ are avoided in listening questions, never actually taught apart
attempts: 1
files: src/data/letters.ts
definition of done: URD-007 stopped `letterPick` from ever pairing two
  same-sound letters (ذ ز ض ظ, and the te/toe, se/seen/swaad and
  baRi-he/choti-he/do-chashmi-he groups) as options, which closes the
  unanswerable-question bug — but none of these letters' `note` fields in
  `letters.ts` teach a learner *which* letter a real word uses. Each note
  only names the collision: zaal's says "another of the 'z' family", zoe's
  says "another of the four ways Urdu spells 'z'". A learner who writes
  Nastaliq directly, or hits a `wordBuild` tray whose randomly-drawn decoy
  happens to be a same-sound rival, has no rule to reach for. Give at least
  the ذ ز ض ظ group a real disambiguation cue — e.g. "ز is native Urdu
  vocabulary, ذ ض ظ are almost always Arabic/Persian loanwords" if that
  holds up, a short high-frequency-word anchor per letter, or similar —
  either in the `note` field or a dedicated teaching moment.
verify: a test or check asserting each of the four ز-sound letters' `note`
  (or an added field) contains a disambiguation cue distinct from merely
  naming the other three letters in the group, plus a manual read of the
  four notes confirming the cue is actually true and usable.

  CURRICULUM CRITIC found two real MAJOR problems round 1 missed:

  1. **The anchor words for `zwaad` and `zoe` were never actually taught.**
     Independently loading `generator.ts`'s `LETTER_CONTEXT_WORD` map — the
     mechanism URD-020/URD-021 built specifically so a letter's lesson
     shows it inside a real, corpus word rather than a decorative one — and
     diffing it against each note's chosen anchor found only 1 of 4
     matched. `ze`'s anchor (زندگی) is the real context word; `zaal`'s
     (ذرا) gets some exposure via sentences elsewhere; `zwaad`'s (ضرب) and
     `zoe`'s (ظرف) appear nowhere in the app outside `letters.ts` itself —
     pure `LetterLabScreen` flashcard trivia, never drilled, never
     reviewed, not what the lesson actually shows for that letter. Telling
     a learner to "anchor to this word" only works if the app gives them a
     reason to remember it.
  2. **`ze`'s note overclaimed a false reverse exclusivity.** It said
     "ذ ض ظ, not this [ze], are the three reserved for words borrowed from
     Arabic" — a two-way claim, and the reverse direction is false: real
     Arabic loanwords already in this corpus (زکوٰۃ/zakaat "alms", عزت/izzat
     "honour", اجازت/ijaazat "permission", معزز/muazziz "honourable") use ز
     precisely because Arabic's own zāy letter is ز. A learner reasoning
     "this word feels Arabic → can't be ز" would guess wrong on real
     vocabulary the app already teaches. The one-way claim — ذ ض ظ never
     occur outside Arabic/Persian loanwords — was independently verified
     and holds with zero exceptions across all 86 relevant words.

  **Round 2** fixed both. `zaal`/`zwaad`/`zoe` re-anchored to
  `LETTER_CONTEXT_WORD.get(id)` directly (کاغذ/kaaghaz "paper",
  ضرورت/zaroorat "necessity", منظر/manzar "scene/view" respectively — all
  three changed from round 1's choices, including `zaal`'s, since the
  context word is the one the letter's own lesson actually uses, not
  whichever one happens to get incidental exposure elsewhere). `ze`'s note
  rewritten to state only the direction that holds. The test rewritten to
  match: it now imports `LETTER_CONTEXT_WORD` directly (a normal TS import,
  not the `load-ts` script hook — this file already runs inside vitest's
  own TS pipeline and doesn't need it) and checks each anchor against it
  rather than the letter's own decorative fields; `ze`'s test asserts the
  note does *not* match a reserved-exclusivity phrase. Confirmed both
  fixes are real regressions the test now catches, not just plausible
  ones: reverted `letters.ts` to the round-1 commit, reran the test, both
  assertions failed with the exact shape each critic named; restored and
