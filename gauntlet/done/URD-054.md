## URD-054 — letterForm asks a learner to tell apart two glyphs that are pixel-identical for non-connecting letters
attempts: 0
files: src/exercises/LetterExercises.tsx, src/data/letters.ts, src/exercises/generator.ts
definition of done: `LetterFormExercise` shows only the bare glyph
  `letter.forms[position]` and asks "which position is this?", graded
  strictly against the one `position` value generated. For the letters
  where `connects: false` (13 of 40 — `alif`, `re`, `Re`, `baRi-ye`, `daal`
  and others), `letter.forms.isolated === letter.forms.initial` and
  `letter.forms.medial === letter.forms.final` as literal identical
  strings (e.g. `alif`: isolated and initial are both "ا") — a
  non-connecting letter has only two visually distinct shapes, not four,
  because it never joins to what follows. A learner shown the `initial`
  glyph for `alif` sees something pixel-identical to the `isolated` glyph,
  with no other context to disambiguate, and answering `isolated` is
  marked flatly wrong. Either accept either visually-identical answer for
  a non-connector, or stop generating the ambiguous position pairs
  (isolated vs initial, medial vs final) for a letter whose `connects` is
  false, asking only the one real distinction (joined vs unjoined) it
  actually has.
verify: a test asserting that for every letter with `connects: false`,
  `letterForm` never generates a question whose correct answer and at
  least one *other* option in `POSITIONS` are visually identical glyphs
  for that letter — or, if the chosen fix is "accept either", a test that
  grading a non-connector's ambiguous pair either way scores correct.
notes: Found by CURRICULUM CRITIC reviewing URD-041. Pre-existing —
  confirmed live already in letter-teaching lessons too (e.g. `l-1-2`,
  "Position practice", built specifically to drill positions, draws
  `alif` at `final` and `medial`, the identical pair) — not introduced by
  URD-041, but newly *reachable in review* by that fix: before it, review's
  one letter slot could never draw `letterForm` at all (see URD-041's own
  history), so review had zero exposure to this ambiguity; after it, the
  ambiguous pairing hit 4 of 10 real `letterForm` draws sampled across
  late-course reviews — roughly the ~32.5% share `connects: false` letters
  hold of the alphabet. Not blocking URD-041, whose own scope (vary the
  kind) is otherwise sound and doesn't touch `letters.ts`'s form data.

RESOLVED, taking the "accept either" branch of the item's own two offered
fixes. `LetterFormExercise` now grades by comparing rendered glyphs
(`letter.forms[picked] === letter.forms[position]`) rather than position
names, in a new pure function `isCorrectPosition` (`letterFormGrading.ts`,
mirroring URD-045's own `isCorrectTap` extraction — a full component
render is disproportionate for one grading decision, and this keeps the
decision unit-testable in isolation). The same comparison drives which
buttons show "correct" after answering, so a learner who picks the
glyph's other real name sees both labels light up, not just the one the
generator happened to pick. A new explanatory line appears only when
that happens ("alif never joins forward, so 'Start' and 'Alone' look the
same here — both are right"), confirmed to render cleanly via a real
harness screenshot (built and torn down per this project's established
technique) rather than assumed from the code alone.

Two real, non-obvious exceptions surfaced while verifying the premise
against the actual data, not assumed from the item's own "isolated ===
initial, medial === final" framing: `hamza` (`connects: false`, but its
own hand-authored `forms` give `medial` and `final` two genuinely
*different* marks — ئ vs ٔ — not the usual collapse) and `baRi-ye`
(`connects: false`, yet all four forms are pairwise distinct — it borrows
`choti-ye`'s connecting shape for `initial`/`medial` and only takes its
own distinctive tail at `isolated`/`final`, a real quirk of where this
letter actually occurs in written Urdu per its own note). `isCorrectPosition`
doesn't need to know either exception exists — comparing glyphs directly,
rather than inferring shape-count from `connects`, gets both right for
free, which is exactly why that approach was chosen over a hand-maintained
per-letter shape table.

New tests in `letterFormGrading.test.ts`: exact-match-only for a connector,
either-twin-accepted for `alif`, still-rejects-the-real-difference for
`alif`, an exhaustive sweep of `isCorrectPosition` against direct glyph
equality for every real letter × every position pair (40 × 4 × 4 = 640
assertions), and both exceptions named directly. Revert-verified: reverting
to plain `picked === position` fails 2 tests with a precise message;
reverting to a naive "collapse initial→isolated, final→medial" rule (the
mistake a less careful fix might make) fails the hamza- and baRi-ye-
specific tests plus the exhaustive sweep, each with a message naming the
exact letter and position pair.

**THE CRITIC: no BLOCKING.** Independently recomputed from the real data
that exactly 13 letters have `connects: false`, of which exactly 2
(hamza, baRi-ye) are hand-authored exceptions to the standard collapse —
confirming no third exception was missed. Ran its own mutation tests
(plain name-equality, and the naive-collapse mistake) and got the same
failures. Proved zero regression risk for all 27 connecting letters two
ways: the exhaustive test, and direct algebra on `connector()`'s
definition (the one same-length form pair can only collide if a letter's
base glyph equals the tatweel character, which no real letter's base
ever is). Rendered the new explanatory sentence for all 13 letters' real
twin pairs via its own throwaway script and found no confusing wording.
Noted, as a nice side-effect rather than a defect: `scripts/soak.js`'s
`answerLetterForm` solver (untouched by this diff) infers a non-connector's
position via `Object.entries(letter.forms).find(...)`, which always
resolves to the *first* matching key in declaration order — under the old
name-based grading this was a latent, roughly-50%-of-cases false-wrong
bug in the soak solver for all 13 non-connecting letters, indistinguishable
from real `wrongOnPurpose` noise. This fix repairs that for free; no
change to `soak.js` was needed or made.

Two real MINOR findings, both fixed before shipping: the doc comment's
