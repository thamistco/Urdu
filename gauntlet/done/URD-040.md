## URD-040 — Review lessons never touch the grammar concepts or sentences that name their own unit
attempts: 0
files: src/lib/review.ts, src/exercises/generator.ts
definition of done: `taughtByLessons` (`lib/review.ts`) only counts
  `kind: 'letters'` and `kind: 'vocab'` lessons; `itemsOf` (`generator.ts`)
  returns `[]` for grammar, sentence, reading and dialogue exercises by
  design, since none of those are SRS-gradable. Concretely,
  rev-saying-who-you-are (u4, "Saying Who You Are") draws its entire review
  from `V('rooms')`/`V('adjectives')` and never once touches `g-pronouns` or
  `g-to-be` — the two grammar concepts the unit is named for and organized
  around. Give a unit review some way to touch the grammar concepts (and
  ideally the sentence-building practice) its own lessons taught, not only
  its vocabulary.
verify: a test asserting a review whose unit includes a grammar lesson
  (e.g. rev-saying-who-you-are) generates at least one exercise referencing
  that unit's own `conceptId`, once a mechanism for it exists.
notes: Found by CURRICULUM CRITIC reviewing URD-016, as a bonus while
  checking whether review content matches "what the unit was actually
  about." Pre-existing — grammar/sentence content has never fed the
  SRS/review system, unrelated to URD-016's own change — but a real gap
  worth its own item rather than folding into a scoping fix that was never
  about which *kinds* of lesson a review can draw from.

  RESOLVED, across two critic rounds, both with real findings.

  `conceptsInUnit` (`src/lib/review.ts`) is the grammar-side equivalent of
  `taughtInUnit`'s letters/words: the grammar concept id(s) a unit's own
  `G(...)` lesson(s) taught, `[]` for the common case of a unit with none
  (measured: never above 2 concepts for any real unit). `buildLessonExercises`'s
  review branch reserves that many slots and appends one `grammarDrill`
  exercise per concept. rev-saying-who-you-are now asks about both
  `g-pronouns` and `g-to-be` — the item's own named example — on every
  track, with the total exercise count unchanged.

  **Round 1 critic findings, both fixed:**

  CURRICULUM CRITIC — MAJOR: the concept-exercise pick was always
  `c.drills[0]`, so a multi-drill concept (`g-to-be` has 3) showed the
  literal same prompt/blank/answer on every replay of the same review,
  forever — directly contradicting the staleness principle URD-039 (the
  item immediately before this one) had just adopted for words and
  letters via its `visit` counter. Fixed by rotating the starting drill
  by `visit % c.drills.length`, still falling back through the rest in
  rotated order for a drill that doesn't render on the current track
  (Roman, per URD-035). Verified directly: `g-to-be`'s pick now cycles
  `d1 → d2 → d3 → d1` across visits 0-3.

  THE CRITIC — BLOCKING: reserving the concept budget by shrinking the
  pool `due` itself was capped against silently dropped real,
  scheduler-flagged due items whenever the due queue alone already
  reached `lesson.size` — the *ordinary* state `dueBudget`'s own contract
  produces, not a rare edge case. Reproduced live: a full 22-item due
  queue for rev-saying-who-you-are dropped two due words, contradicting
  this function's own "due items keep their place at the front" comment
  and `check:srs`'s "the words you got wrong come back first" guarantee.
  Fixed: `refCap` now never falls below `due.length`; the concept budget
  only ever eats into the filler/top-up pool, and a fully-saturated due
  queue correctly skips the concept exercise(s) that don't fit that visit
  rather than dropping a due item or exceeding `lesson.size`. An honest
  trade, not an unconditional guarantee — the item's own definition of
  done never asked for one, and dropping overdue material to make room
  for non-SRS-gradable grammar practice is worse than occasionally
  skipping the practice. Verified with a realistic simulated due queue
  (drawn from `taughtUpTo`, not arbitrary corpus ids): every one of 22 due
  items survives, concept absent that visit; a 17-item (non-saturated)
  queue still surfaces both concepts, due items intact.

  THE CRITIC also filed one MAJOR (silent under-fill if every drill of a
  concept ever failed to render — not live today, no real concept hits
  it) and two MINOR (concept exercises always land at the fixed end
  position; a defensive `Math.max(0, ...)` guarding a case unreachable in
  today's data) as notes, not blocking.

  **Round 2** (THE CRITIC re-dispatched specifically to verify round 1's
  fixes, not a fresh review): confirmed both the BLOCKING and CURRICULUM
  CRITIC's MAJOR genuinely resolved, by direct re-execution rather than
  trusting the shipped tests alone. Surfaced one new latent MINOR: the
  survives-oversized-input guarantee (more due refs than `lesson.size`)
  held only via the interaction of two separate truncation points in the
  function — correct today, but implicit. Hardened by capping `due`
  explicitly at `lesson.size` at the point it's defined: a no-op for real
  behavior (no caller sends more than `lesson.size` due refs today,
  `dueBudget('review', size) === size`), but now a structural guarantee
  rather than one dependent on reasoning about two separate truncation
  points, or on `LessonScreen`'s own behavior never changing. The two
  round-1 MINORs (fixed end position; unreachable-today guard) remain
  filed-forward, unchanged, not addressed by this item.

  Every fix verified as a real regression its test catches, not just
  plausible: reverted `generator.ts` alone (keeping each new test) for
  both the stale-drill and dropped-due-item fixes in turn; each failed
  with the exact shape its critic named; restored and reconfirmed clean.
