## URD-025 — A sentence-derived climb should lean on sentenceBuild, not recognition
attempts: 0
files: src/exercises/generator.ts
definition of done: two call sites now share the identical `turn =
  (round + idx) % 3` climb over `SENTENCE_WORDS` — the `sentences` branch,
  and (as of URD-024's grammar follow-up) the `grammar` branch's
  sentence-reinforcement climb. Both give a sentence one `meaningPick`
  turn, one `wordFromMeaning` turn, one `sentenceBuild` turn — 2 of 3 reps
  spent on whole-sentence recognition (see `SENTENCE_WORDS`: a sentence is
  shown and judged as one opaque string, never segmented) and only 1 of 3
  forcing the learner to place its words in order. `sentences.ts`'s own
  header and gauntlet/BENCHMARKS.md both name sentence-building
  specifically as what makes this app teach word order "far better than
  any explanation" and as the reason to choose Harf over Duolingo/Drops —
  the current ratio gives the weaker exercise the majority of the reps at
  both call sites. Rebalance so sentenceBuild is not the minority turn —
  either two sentenceBuild passes to one recognition pass, or a fourth
  round, whichever keeps the sitting inside check:shape's 3-8 minute band
  — fixed once, ideally by factoring the shared climb into one function
  both branches call, so the two cannot drift out of sync again.

  RESOLVED: a new shared `sentenceReinforceClimb` function (`generator.ts`)
  replaces the duplicated logic at both call sites. Every sentence now gets
  1 `meet`, 2 `recall`, 2 `produce` turns (5 total, not 3) — `sentenceBuild`
  ties `wordFromMeaning` for the single most frequent kind, up from tied
  for least with both. `meaningPick` (the purely passive recognition kind)
  is the one that gave up a turn, since `wordFromMeaning` (retrieval from
  meaning) is already a step closer to production and was kept level with
  `sentenceBuild` rather than cut.

  This is NOT the item's other named option ("two sentenceBuild passes to
  one recognition pass," i.e. produce as the outright 2:1 majority) — that
  was tried first, as a 4-round 2:1:1 climb, and reverted after
  `check:shape`'s own share rule failed it directly: `MAX_SHARE = 0.4`
  caps every kind, including `produce`, at 40%, and 2:1:1 puts produce at
  50%. Worked out exactly, not estimated: making `produce` strictly
  outnumber BOTH `meet` and `recall` individually while every kind stays
  ≤40% share requires at least 10 turns per sentence (4 produce / 3 meet /
  3 recall is the cheapest integer split satisfying both constraints,
  verified by brute-force, not just by hand) — more than triple today's 3,
  which would push real lessons well past the 8-minute ceiling (measured:
  an 8-sentence lesson at 10 turns each is 12 minutes). 2:1:2 is the
  documented, deliberate compromise: the maximum shift toward the item's
  goal achievable without breaking a check the item's own verify command
  runs.

  Both `sentences` and `grammar` call sites now use the identical ratio via
  the one shared function — including grammar, where a prior review had
  flagged a legitimate question (does `grammarDrill`'s own construction
  test make a heavier `sentenceBuild` redundant there?) and asked that the
  answer be stated explicitly rather than defaulted silently. Answered in
  the grammar call site's own comment: `grammarDrill`'s options are
  already-correctly-inflected forms, so it tests which form is right;
  `sentenceBuild`'s tiles are also already correctly inflected, and what it
  tests is word ORDER, a skill `grammarDrill` never touches — the two are
  complementary, not redundant, so doubling `sentenceBuild` there isn't
  padding.
