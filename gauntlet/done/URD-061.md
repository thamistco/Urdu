## URD-061 — the discrimination skill is practised twice in one lesson and never again
attempts: 0
files: src/exercises/generator.ts, src/lib/review.ts
definition of done: `letterContrast` (URD-047) is emitted only by the
  `letters` branch. Measured across the whole course: 33 instances, all in
  `kind: 'letters'` lessons, zero anywhere else — and driving the first 20
  review lessons with all 40 letters due emits `letterForm` 152 /
  `letterPick` 150 / `letterTrace` 153 and `letterContrast` 0. So for a pair
  like ص/ض or ط/ظ — near-identical shapes, and for a beginner near-identical
  sounds — a learner is asked to tell them apart twice, about six exercises
  apart, on a single day, and the SRS never asks again. Every other skill in
  the app is spaced; this is the only kind that is not. Make `letterContrast`
  reachable from review, so the discrimination survives the lesson that
  taught it.
verify: a test asserting a review lesson with confusable letters due can emit
  `letterContrast`, and that across the course's reviews every letter with a
  confusable partner meets one at least once after its teaching lesson.
notes: Found by CURRICULUM CRITIC reviewing URD-047 and judged its most
  serious finding, against BENCHMARKS.md's "enough times, in enough different
  shapes, to survive until tomorrow" — this shape has no tomorrow. Not fixed
  in URD-047 because reaching review means `reviewLetterPool` has to know
  about buckets and about which letters are co-known, which is real design
  rather than a call-site change.

RESOLVED. Inside the `review` branch of `buildLessonExercises`
(`generator.ts`), a due letter with a confusable mate can now render as
`letterContrastExercise` instead of its usual `letterExerciseAt`-chosen
kind — `due` is the SRS scheduler already flagging the letter as needing
reinforcement, the same moment the word side of this same loop spends on
its own harder recall/produce demands. Mates are drawn from
`taughtUpTo(lesson.id).letters` (the whole course-so-far, not one
lesson's own small group the way the `letters` branch's own
`confusableMatesIn` call scopes it), deduped by id, and — after THE
CRITIC's BLOCKING finding below — restricted to `known` for a lesson
placed nowhere on the path. Capped at exactly one `letterContrast` per
review generation, chosen by rotating through every eligible due letter
with a mate rather than always the first (after CURRICULUM CRITIC's
MAJOR finding below).

Dispatched both THE CRITIC and CURRICULUM CRITIC via the WIP-commit
review pattern (reset after both reported), each finding a real,
independently-verified defect that the first version's own tests could
not catch.

**THE CRITIC (BLOCKING, fixed): an off-path review (Daily Review) could
show a confusable mate the learner had never actually met.**
`taughtUpTo(lessonId)` returns the *entire* course's letters for a lesson
id placed nowhere on the path — true of `practice-review`, the synthetic
Daily Review screen. `fallbackReviewRefs` two screens down had already
hit this identical trap once (THE CRITIC, URD-017) and fixed it by
restricting to `known` off-path; the first version of this item's new
code reused the same off-path lesson id without reapplying that
restriction. Reproduced live, independently, by both THE CRITIC and me:
a learner graded on `re` alone (e.g. from backing out of `l-3` early —
`useSessionGradeFlush` flushes on unmount, not only on completion, so a
partial group can reach SRS) opened Daily Review and was offered a
`letterContrast` for `re` against `Re`/`ze`/`zhe` — three glyphs never
taught, as answer choices, on the one screen meant to reinforce only
what was already met. Fixed by computing `onPathForContrast` the same
way `fallbackReviewRefs`'s own `onPath` does, and filtering
`taughtUpTo`'s letters to `known` whenever the lesson isn't on the path.
Re-verified against the exact repro (now falls through to the normal
letter kind) and against the positive case (fires once the learner has
actually met the whole bucket); a dedicated regression test added and
revert-verified.

**CURRICULUM CRITIC (MAJOR, fixed): "first due letter with a mate wins
the slot" permanently starved some bucket members, for any bucket of 3
or more.** `dueQueue` sorts due cards soonest-overdue-first, a stable
order that barely moves once two cards are graded identically — so
whichever member's due timestamp happened to edge ahead kept winning the
one contrast slot every time the group came due together, for as long as
that lead held. CURRICULUM CRITIC simulated this against the real
corpus's two 3+-member buckets (`daal`/`Daal`/`zaal`;
`re`/`Re`/`ze`/`zhe`) using this app's own SM-2 across an 8-year, ~19-
review horizon at perfect recall: `zaal` and `ze`/`zhe` never won even
once. I confirmed the mechanism directly and independently (reversing a
due queue's own order flips the winner, on the identical lesson/letters).
Fixed by choosing among *every* letter in `due` with a mate — not just
the first — via `(reviewIndex + visit) % eligible.length`, the same
"vary across different real reviews and repeat visits" technique
`letterTurnOffset`/`posOffset` already use elsewhere in this file for
exactly this reason. Re-simulated the same two buckets with realistic
per-lesson visit tracking (my first re-simulation attempt used an
unrealistic global visit counter and wrongly looked skewed — caught and
corrected before treating it as confirmed): every member of both buckets
now wins the slot repeatedly at perfect recall (roughly even 2-4 wins
each per ~8-10 total encounters over 8000 simulated days) and no member
is ever starved to zero at 90% recall either (worst case 13 of 193
encounters, never 0). Two new tests added: one walks every real review
lesson after a letter's teaching lesson (not just the first, since the
rotation no longer guarantees the tested letter wins its very first
encounter) and asserts it wins at least one; one directly checks both
real 3+-member buckets each see every member win the slot across the
course's real review lessons. The original "first review only" version
of the coverage test was itself shown to be a false-confidence test —
CURRICULUM CRITIC proved it would pass regardless of fairness, since its
due queue always listed the tested letter first — and was rewritten
before being trusted.

Also confirmed, independently, before treating either critic's finding as
real: THE CRITIC's dedup claim (removing the `Map`-based dedup on
`taughtLetterObjs` reproduces the exact duplicate-glyph failure) and cap
claim (removing the one-per-review cap reproduces `check:shape`'s 84%/
19-in-a-row measurement) both hold under direct mutation.

THE CRITIC also flagged two MINOR points, left as documented rather than
changed: the "never appears on the Roman track" test is trivially true
(it's gated by the pre-existing `teachesScript`-based `l` lookup, not by
anything URD-061 added) rather than exercising this item's own new code
specifically; and the exactly-one-per-review cap means, for a
persistently-co-due pair, the later-favoured member still gets somewhat
fewer discrimination checks than the earlier one even with rotation —
self-correcting over time (grading reschedules the card), not a starve-
to-zero problem the way the un-rotated version was. CURRICULUM CRITIC's
note that a letter which always wins the contrast slot when due loses
that visit's own position-form (`letterExerciseAt`) exposure is real but
narrow — the same substitution trade the un-rotated version already
made, now spread fairly across a bucket rather than concentrated on one
letter, and does not reopen URD-060's course-wide coverage guarantee
(that guarantee is proven over `letterForm`/`letterTrace`/`letterPick`
sightings in *teaching* lessons, which this item does not touch).
