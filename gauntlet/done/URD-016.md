## URD-016 — A review should mostly review the unit it closes
attempts: 0
files: src/exercises/generator.ts, src/data/units.ts
definition of done: `fallbackReviewRefs` in generator.ts draws uniformly from
  every word and letter taught up to the review, with no weighting toward the
  unit it is attached to. Measured on real generated output with nothing due:
  rev-gender-and-number (u6) draws 0-5% of its words from u6; rev-the-wider-
  world (u39) draws 3-5% from u39. A review should draw mostly from its own
  unit, falling back to the wider course only when the unit cannot fill it —
  which happens: rev-your-first-readings closes a unit with zero vocabulary
  lessons and needs the fallback for all of it. A test asserts a review whose
  unit has enough taught words draws at least half of them from that unit.
verify: npm test -- src/lib/review.test.ts
notes: Found by the CURRICULUM CRITIC on URD-010, independently confirmed by
  THE CRITIC from a different angle: two-thirds of reviews (26 of 39) are
  governed purely by REVIEW_MIN rather than by the unit's actual size, because
  most units don't teach 66 words. That is the same problem URD-010's commit
  set out to fix — a flat number wherever the review sits — relocated from a
  flat 9 to a flat 22. Scoping selection to the closing unit and sizing the
  review off what the unit can actually supply are the same piece of work;
  do not fix one without the other. Also carries the curriculum critic's
  MINOR finding that letter/word alternation is now a rigid fixed cadence,
  worth a second look while this is open.

  RESOLVED: new `src/lib/review.ts` exports `taughtInUnit(lessonId)` —
  words/letters taught by lessons in the SAME unit as a lesson, via `UNITS`,
  not the whole course-so-far — and `prioritizedPool(tiers, seedBase)`, which
  concatenates pools in priority order, deduping, and shuffling *within* each
  tier independently rather than shuffling the whole concatenation together.
  That independence is the actual fix: shuffling everything as one list lets
  a huge low-priority tier dilute a tiny high-priority one purely by chance,
  which is exactly how the old flat course-wide pool drowned out a unit's own
  dozen words. `reviewWordPool`/`reviewLetterPool` combine these into the
  full tiered logic (unit-known, unit-taught, course-wide-known, course-wide-
  taught, corpus), and `generator.ts`'s `fallbackReviewRefs` now calls them
  instead of building one flat pool. Measured directly on real generated
  output: rev-gender-and-number and rev-the-wider-world now draw 100% of
  their words from their own unit (up from the reported 0% and ~5%);
  rev-your-first-readings (a unit with zero vocabulary lessons) correctly
  still falls through to course-wide, exactly as the item's own note
  describes.

  Building the check itself surfaced a real regression in its own first
  draft, found by running the full check suite rather than just the new
  test: treating "known within the unit" and "taught course-wide, ungraded"
  as two tiers in the same priority list meant that once the unit's own
  known words ran out, the pool quietly widened to *any* taught material —
  reintroducing the exact thing `check-srs.js`'s pre-existing "a review with
  nothing due never asks about a word outside what the learner has been
  graded on" exists to catch. Fixed: whether `known` restricts the pool at
  all is an all-or-nothing decision — once anything reachable from the
  review has been graded, the whole pool stays restricted to graded ids
  (unit-known ordered first); only when nothing anywhere is graded yet does
  it widen to the full taught pool.

  THE CRITIC found BLOCKING: that restriction was still decided per type —
  the word pool looked only at `known ∩ courseWideWords`, the letter pool
  only at `known ∩ courseWideLetters`. `known` is a single flat set of every
  id graded regardless of type, and a learner graded on many words but zero
  letters is reachable, not synthetic: studying the Roman track (which drops
  letter lessons from the path entirely) for a stretch, then switching to
  Script or Both, reaches exactly this state on the very next review.
  Deciding the letter side alone found nothing known there and widened to
  *every letter ever taught* — reproduced live: 12 of 16 exercises were
  letters the learner had never once been shown. Fixed: both pools now key
  off whether *anything*, of either type, has been graded, so a learner
  known on words alone gets a correctly empty (never manufactured) letter
  pool instead of a flooded one. Also MAJOR (filed forward, not fixed
  inline): the per-tier shuffle seed is keyed on `lessonId` alone, so once a
  learner has graded every word in a small unit, the fallback always slices
  off the identical subset of that unit's words forever — pre-existing,
  deliberate content-selection convention project-wide, made visible rather
  than introduced by shrinking the pool this fix draws from.

  CURRICULUM CRITIC (never blocks): confirmed the word-side scoping claim
  exactly (100% in-unit wherever a unit teaches vocabulary), but measured
  that *overall* in-unit share (words and letters together, what a learner
  actually experiences) averages only 53.5% and is ≤50% for 30 of 39
  reviews — the pre-existing, unconditional 50/50 letter/word split
  colliding with every letter lesson living in units 1-9, so every review
  from u10 onward permanently reaches for pre-alphabet letters regardless of
  how well the unit's own vocabulary could otherwise fill it. This is the
  exact problem already queued as URD-017 ("a review's letter share should
  decay once the alphabet is behind it"), found independently here and
  folded into that item's notes rather than filed as a duplicate. Also found
  the same deterministic-seed staleness THE CRITIC did, independently. Bonus
  finding: review lessons never touch the grammar concepts or sentences that
  give a unit its name (`rev-saying-who-you-are` never touches `g-pronouns`
  or `g-to-be`) — pre-existing, filed as new URD-040.

  Verified via induced failure three times over: reverting the whole
  unit-scoping approach reproduced the item's own reported 0%/~5% in-unit
  share exactly; reverting just the known-restriction reproduced the
  ungraded-word leak `check-srs.js` exists to catch; reverting just the
  cross-type joint-known check reproduced THE CRITIC's exact 12-of-16
  never-taught-letters finding. All three restored and reconfirmed clean.
