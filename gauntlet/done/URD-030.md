## URD-030 — The grammar climb's distractor pool doesn't know which concept it's reinforcing
attempts: 0
files: src/exercises/generator.ts
definition of done: `reinforcePool` in the grammar branch (feeding
  `meaningPick`/`wordFromMeaning` distractors) is every `SENTENCE_WORDS`
  entry at the concept's CEFR level, drawn with no concept-awareness at
  all — the same flat, unweighted draw `distractorsFor` already does for
  plain vocabulary. Measured across all 290 meaningPick/wordFromMeaning
  exercises the grammar climb currently emits: only 26.9% (78 of 290) have
  even one distractor tagged to the same grammar concept as the correct
  answer. The other 73.1% offer a correct sentence (e.g. a comparative)
  against three topically unrelated options (e.g. "I like listening to
  music" / "You should rest" / "The doctor gave medicine") — answerable by
  topic/vocabulary recognition alone, without parsing the comparative
  marker, passive auxiliary, or plural ending the lesson is actually
  about. Bias the distractor draw toward same-level, different-concept
  sentences that are near-misses in form, so a correct answer requires
  noticing the construction, not just the topic.
verify: a script measuring same-concept-distractor rate across all
  generated grammar-climb meaningPick/wordFromMeaning exercises reports it
  well above today's 26.9% (100% is not required — same-concept sentences
  may not exist for every draw at small pool sizes — but the flat,
  unweighted draw should no longer be the default).

  RESOLVED: added `concept?: string` to the `Word` type (`src/data/
  words.ts`), mirroring the existing `register` field's precedent for a
  synthetic sentence-as-word field, and populated it in `SENTENCE_WORDS`'s
  mapping (`generator.ts`) from `Sentence.concept`. Added an optional
  `preferred?: Word[]` parameter to `distractorsFor`, tried before the
  main pool, defaulting to `[]` so every existing caller draws exactly as
  before. `sentenceReinforceClimb` computes, per sentence, the other
  same-concept sentences at that level as its preferred pool and threads
  it through `wordExercise`.

  Measured result: the same-concept-distractor rate rose from 19.6%
  (172/876, re-measured against the current corpus) to 66.7% (584/876).

  A significant limitation was found and filed forward, not hidden:
  `meaningPick` specifically stays at 0% same-concept hits, both before
  and after this fix (292/292 exercises). Root cause: `meaningPick` sets
  `distinctCue: true`, and `distractorsFor` seeds `usedCues` with the
  *correct answer's own* cue before considering any candidate; `cueOf`
  (`src/data/art.ts`) falls through to `emo:${word.emoji}` for any
  sentence-derived word, and every sentence in the course shares the
  identical literal `'📝'` emoji — so any sentence-vs-sentence pairing
  collides with the seed regardless of pool composition. The entire
  improvement is attributable to `wordFromMeaning` alone, which reaches
  100% coverage wherever a concept has ≥1 other usable same-level tagged
  sentence. Filed as URD-050 (new queue item) rather than fixed here —
  this item's own scope is the distractor *pool*, not the cue system
  `meaningPick`'s picture-question design depends on.

  Added `scripts/check-grammar-distractors.js` (new script, wired into

  `check-shape.js`'s measurement-not-gate precedent (not wired into
  `check:all` or CI).

  CURRICULUM CRITIC reviewed and found two real, actioned issues:

  - MAJOR: an uncapped `consider(preferred)` saturated every distractor
    slot whenever a concept had enough near-misses — measured, 580 of 584
    `wordFromMeaning` exercises had ALL three distractors same-concept,
    not "at least one." A recall question had collapsed into "tell these
    four near-identical sentences apart" with no option left that just
    looks different. FIXED: capped `preferred`'s own contribution at
    `DISTRACTORS - 1`, and excluded `preferred`'s members from the main
    pool's own `consider()` call (without the exclusion, a big enough
    `preferred` pool could refill the reserved slot from itself anyway).
    Verified via a full distribution count across all 876 exercises:
    0:292, 1:0, 2:584, 3:0 — never fully saturated, every hit has exactly
    one non-concept anchor.
  - MINOR/diagnostic: `check-grammar-distractors.js`'s own comment
    misattributed every concept landing at ~67% to "thin tagged-sentence
    pools" — g-postpositions (21 tagged, the largest pool in the course)
    sits at the identical rate as g-plurals (4 tagged), disproving that.
    FIXED: the script now reports the exercise-kind breakdown (meaningPick
    0%, wordFromMeaning 100%-with-cap) and the full same-concept-count
    distribution directly, and the misleading comment is corrected.
