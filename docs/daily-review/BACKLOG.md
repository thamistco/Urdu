| P-001 | **How Qaaf is paid for: a daily free allowance with a trial at the limit** (the owner raised the Drops model). Free forever: the whole alphabet, all review, and 2 new lessons a day. Premium: unlimited lessons and a family plan. Hitting the daily limit offers a 7-day Premium trial. Starting prices ~$9.99/month, ~$69.99/year, a family plan near $119.99, lifetime ~$149.99. Built once payments exist (Q-002). | 2026-10-06    | **Learner:** a daily allowance builds the habit that education apps lose (2 to 3% kept at day 30); counting lessons rather than minutes never cuts a lesson off halfway. **Parent:** a course that is never fully locked earns a family's trust. **Business, against:** free-with-limits converts about 2.1% of trials against 10.7% for a hard paywall (RevenueCat 2026); the trial at the limit is there to win some of that back, and it is a bet. **Competitors:** Drops gives 5 free minutes a day ($69.99/yr), Duolingo meters through hearts, Ling uses a trial then a paywall. **Engineering:** needs payments and native builds first. **Learners of other apps:** a paywall straight after the first lesson is among Ling's top complaints, which supports keeping the alphabet free. |
| P-002 | **Lead with "You speak it. Now read it."** in the store listing and on the sign-in screen, aimed at heritage adults who speak Urdu but cannot read it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | 2026-10-06    | **Heritage learner:** names their exact gap. **Beginner with no Urdu, against:** may read as "not for me"; the line under it must still welcome them. **Competitors:** no Urdu app claims script-first for speakers. To check on day two: whether it sits in the sign-in screen's tight dark band without breaking check:scenery.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| P-003 | **Say it back.** After each dialogue and reading line plays, the learner repeats it aloud (shadowing). Optional: record yourself and play the two back to back, kept on the phone, never uploaded, never scored.                                                                                                                                                                                                        | 2026-10-06    | **Learner:** speaking is the top gap learners name in every big app, and Qaaf has none. **Research:** shadowing improved pronunciation and listening in published studies, in sessions of 10 to 15 minutes. **Against, privacy and trust:** the privacy policy promises no microphone; recording must be opt-in, on-device only, and the policy updated to say exactly that. **Against, competitors:** Ling's speech scoring fails for users, so no automatic scoring. **Disabled learner:** speaking must never be required to pass a lesson. **Engineering:** microphone recording on web and native (expo-av), a permission prompt, one new step type.                                                                                                                                       |
| P-004 | **Read faster.** Short reading-speed rounds for the script: known words flash up, the learner reads and taps to hear and check, and a words-per-minute figure tracks progress towards the 45 to 60 that research ties to fluent reading. Offered first to learners who said at setup that they already speak Urdu.                                                                                                      | 2026-10-06    | **Heritage speaker:** decoding is their only barrier; speed is what turns knowing the letters into reading. **Research:** automatic letter and word recognition is what fluent Arabic-script reading rests on. **Against, anxiety and access:** a clock can stress learners and exclude some disabled ones, so timing is optional, never a pass mark, and never costs hearts. **Beginner:** comes after the alphabet, using only words already taught. **Engineering:** reuses existing words and clips; needs a timer and a stored best.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
