# 2026-10-26 · Learning science (area 16): what a speaker already knows, and re-asks

Reviewed by `qaaf-learning` (26 tool calls, about 3 minutes, read-only).

## Q-093: the words of the lessons a speaker skips

Choosing "I already speak or understand it" skips 50 beginner word lessons,
501 words, by self-report (not the quiz). Only grading a word in a lesson
adds it to `learnedWords`, and every count read only that, so a speaker saw 0
everywhere.

**Decided and built:** the skipped lessons' words are known by ear, worked
out from `skippedLessons` when read; no review cards; achievements stay on
practised words; Read faster uses them once every letter is met. Evidence:
heritage speakers usually have the spoken words and not the literacy
(Polinsky and Kagan 2007; Montrul 2010); spacing protects newly learned items
(Cepeda et al. 2006), which a speaker's everyday words are not; decoding words
already known by ear is how word reading becomes automatic (Share 1995; Ehri
2005; the link to Read faster is judgement). Measured: a speaker's Read faster
pool is 3 words after l-1, 22 after l-3, 498 after l-8.

Second check (`qaaf-critic`, data and engineering, an over-claiming learner,
the case against): **SHIP**, one major fixed the same night (Profile's "501
Words" beside "0/40 Letters" and an achievement at "0 / 10" with nothing
saying which count was which), four minors fixed. Left: a learner who
over-claimed has no way back from Read faster's rounds but the reveal.

**Against:** the skip rests on self-report, so counts can be inflated;
that is why Profile says "Words known" and achievements stay on practise.

## P-085's re-asks, made a real second retrieval (proposed)

In early lessons every re-ask was "How do you say it?", easier than the miss.
Proposed mapping, harder or equal to the miss and with a different cue:
picture or meaning missed → hear it and pick (listenTap), else type or build;
listenTap missed → meaningPick; recall missed → build (script, letters
taught) or type (Roman), else listenTap; typed → build, else listenTap; built →
listenTap. Harder retrieval is remembered better (Pyc and Rawson 2009; Bjork
1994), and a failed attempt with feedback still helps (Kornell, Hays and
Bjork 2009). Against: a harder question straight after a miss risks a second
failure. Not built tonight; recorded against P-085.

## What this is not evidence for

Whether any speaker's self-report is accurate; retention; the 3, 22 and 498
are words in the pool, not words read.
