# 2026-10-18 · Content quality (area 8): vocabulary and sentences

Swept by `qaaf-learning` (17 tool calls, about 15 minutes, read-only), loading
the data with scratch scripts. Read once each: all 2,276 words, all 256
sentences, and 143 lines across 17 readings and 12 dialogues, 2,675 items.
Three checks were scripted: the Roman of every Urdu word in a sentence
against the vocabulary; every ں, ڑ, ٹ and ڈ against ñ, ṛ, ṭ and ḍ; and items
that share an English gloss. Platts and Rekhta are cited from memory, not
looked up tonight.

## Certain, and fixed tonight (899b12b)

- **افق "horizon" was taught twice** (w-ufuq and w-ufuq2). The copy is retired
  onto the original, so a learner who met it keeps its progress; the word
  count is 2,275 everywhere it is stated.
- **w-paint "rang-rogan"**: غ is "gh" everywhere else, now rang-roghan.
- **s-147 and s-9 were both "The book is on the table"**, so an English prompt
  had two right answers. s-147 (کے اوپر) is now "The book is on top of the
  table".

## Certain, not fixed tonight

- **ہنس is "hañsi" in w-hansi and "hansna" in w-hansna.** One has to change,
  and which depends on the nasal rule below.
- **The nasal ñ rule is not kept.** `translit.ts` says ñ marks nasalisation:
  نہیں is "nahiñ", آئیں "aayeñ"; but میں "main" (34 times), ہیں "hain" (34), ہوں
  "hoon" (16), and جاؤں, کروں, پڑھوں drop it; the vocabulary has "kaise
  jaaooñ" where s-19 has "jaaoon". Proposed (P-092): keep "main" and "hain"
  as written exceptions (every Pakistani writes them so in Roman Urdu, and
  "main" stays apart from "meñ"), fix the real slips, and add a unit test
  that every ں maps to ñ unless the word is on the exception list. Against:
  "main" teaches an English n in the most frequent word of the course. A
  research change, so it waits for its second check.
- **29 words share a gloss with nothing to tell them apart** (P-039, still
  open): کب/جب "when", ہارنا/کھونا "to lose" (a game, an object), قد/اونچائی
  "height", ہوش/شعور "consciousness".

- Meaning: w-shaz کبھی کبھار is "rarely", but means "occasionally"; w-murgh

- Register (judgement): آشپز (Pakistanis say باورچی), خاکستری (سرمئی),
  غزل گائیک (غزل گلوکار).
- Spelling: w-bhow بھؤں, probably بھوں.
- Readings: r-8 میرے استاد بہت اچھے ہیں glossed "My teachers", the honorific
  for one teacher; r-2 opens صبح میں بازار گیا, which a beginner reads as
  "in the morning in…"; "میں صبح بازار گیا" is clearer.
