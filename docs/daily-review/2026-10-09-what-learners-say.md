## What Urdu learners say

- **Nastaliq against Naskh, a new angle.** A learner who understands spoken
  Urdu reads Naskh slowly but "can't even decipher" Nastaliq; commenters advise
  typing a sentence in Naskh, switching it to Nastaliq and drilling the shapes
  that change. A second r/Urdu post complains that Google and others default to
  Naskh. Two threads.
- **Reading without vowel marks.** One learner reads with harakat and struggles
  without; guides put newspapers at the advanced end. One voice, but a known
  hurdle.
- **Transliteration that does not match the script:** reported of UrduPod101,
  which admitted errors; two reviews also say "too much English" and little past
  beginner level.
- **Synthetic voices.** No Urdu learner was found complaining of one. Rivals
  sell "native speaker audio" as the difference (Ling, Mango, uTalk); a small
  study (36 students) found learners prefer human voices but often cannot tell
  them apart; a TTS vendor admits unmarked Urdu makes synthesis guess the
  vowels. Qaaf's audio is synthesised, and harakat reach only the voice
  generator.
- **Urdu and Hindi:** treated on Reddit as two registers of one language; no
  complaint found that an Urdu app taught Hindi words. Weak.
- **Roman Urdu for texting:** only tutors' advice (Roman for WhatsApp, the
  script by months 2 to 3, essential for poetry and news). Weak.
- **Poetry:** real demand, served by Rekhta's courses, a McGill ghazal class
  and r/Urdu members' own ghazals. Qaaf's onboarding offers "Poetry, faith…",
  yet the course has almost none.
- **Parents:** children's qaida apps have under ten ratings each, carry ads and
  give no privacy details (as on 10-08).

## Proposals

- **P-023: a font switch round.** Re-read lines already known in Naskh and in a
  second Nastaliq face. For: heritage learners and anyone reading the web, and
  it is exactly r/Urdu's method. Against: a second style can confuse beginners;
  fonts add download size and must be bundled, since `check:controls` forbids
  font CDNs; one or two threads of evidence.
- **P-024: a human ear on the voices.** A native or heritage speaker checks the
  heteronym clips, and P-020 gets a "sounds wrong" category. For: beginners
  cannot hear errors, so they trust the voice entirely. Against: a person's
  time (the owner's call), no learner complaint found, and
  `check:voice-fidelity` already checks that each clip says its text.
- **P-025: one couplet per advanced unit,** a sher from Ghalib or Mir, recorded,
  with a gloss. For: learners who chose "poetry" get almost none. Against:
  Persian-heavy vocabulary past the course, TTS may flatten the metre, Faiz is
  in copyright until about 2034, and few beginners reach it.
