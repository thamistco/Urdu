# 2026-10-06 · Market and competitors

First night of the daily review. Area 1: who else teaches Urdu, what they
charge, where they are weak, and who Qaaf's learners are. Research only; no
code changed tonight. Two sites were blocked by this environment's network
policy (actualfluency.com, boli.it.com), so some competitor detail below comes
from search summaries rather than the pages themselves, and says so.

## What we found

### Nobody big teaches Urdu to English speakers

- **Duolingo** has no Urdu course for English speakers. What it is building is
  the reverse, an English course for Urdu speakers, expected around late summer
  2026 according to people tracking its code
  ([duolingoguides](https://duolingoguides.com/duolingo-new-languages-2027/),
  [duolingodata](https://duolingodata.com/dailynews.html)). Babbel has no Urdu
  either ([Ling blog](https://ling-app.com/da/blog/er-der-ikke-urdu-paa-duolingo/)).
- The apps that do teach Urdu are generalists with Urdu as one of 30 to 60
  languages: **Ling**, **Mondly**, **uTalk**, **50 Languages**, plus
  **UrduPod101** (audio lessons) and **Talkpal** (AI chat)
  ([Ling's own list](https://ling-app.com/blog/urdu-learning-app/),
  [langoly](https://www.langoly.com/urdu-learning-apps/),
  [uTalk](https://utalk.com/plans/urdu)).
- uTalk sells on native-speaker recordings, "while many competitors use
  artificial voices" ([uTalk](https://store.utalk.com/languages/urdu)).
  Qaaf's voices are synthesised (Google Chirp 3 HD). That is a point against
  us a reviewer could make, and is in the backlog.

### A new, crowded-looking niche: heritage families

Three products are aimed squarely at diaspora parents and children:

- **BOLI**, "the world's first heritage language app": an AI "Nana" to talk to,
  family quests, stories; founded after watching a Pakistani-Canadian child lose
  Urdu. Sells "connection, not education"
  ([search summary](https://boli.it.com/); the page itself was blocked here).
- **KidsBolo**, Urdu for children of families in the West
  ([kidsbolo](https://kidsbolo.com/pages/learn-urdu-online)).
- **Bolnama**, "for the Pakistani diaspora"
  ([site](https://bolnama-marketing-la-a018.bolt.host/)).

None of the three, from what could be read, teaches the script the way Qaaf
does: every letter in all four joining forms, with tracing.

### Who the learners are

- People of Pakistani origin: about **1.66 million in the UK** (2021), **684,000
  in the US** (2023), **303,000 in Canada** (2021)
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_diaspora)).
- Heritage language loss is the recurring worry: one source puts children
  losing the home language at as many as half, through parents' passivity
  rather than choice
  ([urdumom](https://urdumom.com/2017/02/urdu-research/),
  [Traversing Tradition](https://traversingtradition.com/2022/11/09/language-is-a-grandmother-the-long-and-torturous-death-of-urdu-in-america/)).
  Community groups run their own events for it
  ([American Pakistan Foundation, Urdu Bolo](https://americanpakistan.org/new-events/urdu-bolo-preserving-linguistic-ties)).
- Judgement, not data: three groups worth naming separately. Heritage adults
  who speak some Urdu but cannot read it (Qaaf's script-first course and its
  "Do you already know some Urdu?" placement are built for exactly them).
  Parents wanting their children to keep it (BOLI's market; Qaaf has nothing
  for children or families yet). And partners, friends and lovers of Urdu
  poetry and music with no family tie (the Roman track serves them).

### What the subscription data says

From RevenueCat's _State of Subscription Apps 2026_ (115,000 apps)
([report](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education),
[summary](https://www.saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps/)):

- Hard paywalls convert trials to paid at a median **10.7%** by day 35,
  freemium at **2.1%**, about five times lower.
- One-year retention of yearly subscribers is the same either way (27% vs 28%).
- Revenue per install by day 60: **$3.09** hard paywall vs **$0.38** freemium.

Education apps keep about **14 to 15%** of installs on day 1 and **2 to 3%** on
day 30, the lowest of any category; January installs retain better
([Business of Apps / Passion.io roundups](https://www.passion.io/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks-for-creators-course-coaching-apps)).
None of these figures is evidence about Qaaf: we have no installs, no store
presence and no analytics, so we cannot yet say where we sit.

### How small language apps have grown

- **Fluently** (English coaching) reached $70K monthly revenue on TikTok
  content alone, all of it promising to make the viewer "sound more native":
  belonging, not grammar ([TikTok Creative Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/trend_article/online/community-lingo/pad/en)).
- Community channels repeatedly named: language subreddits, Reels and TikTok,
  diaspora Facebook groups and Discords
  ([trykaiwa marketing notes](https://trykaiwa.com/docs/strategy-marketing)).

## What it means for Qaaf

These are recommendations, for the owner to accept or reject.

1. **Positioning.** The one thing nobody else claims: _read Urdu as it is
   actually written_. Every letter in every joining form, Nastaliq throughout,
   tracing, then words, grammar and conversation on top. Lead with the heritage
   adult who can speak but cannot read: "You speak it. Now read it." That is a
   need the big apps do not touch and the heritage-family apps aim past.
2. **The gap to close first is families.** BOLI shows the parent-and-child
   market is real and being chased. Qaaf's course suits an adult; a family plan
   and a parent's view of a child's progress would let it compete there without
   rebuilding the course. Queued.
3. **Price within the field, not below it.** Judgement: around **$69.99/year**,
   **$9.99/month**, a **family plan** near Duolingo's $119.99, and a lifetime
   option because Ling's $149.99 shows learners of rare languages buy them.
   Test before committing; this is a starting hypothesis.
4. **Paywall shape.** The data favours a hard paywall with a trial. Qaaf's
   alphabet units are its strongest demo, so the likeliest shape is: the
   alphabet free, everything after it behind a 7-day trial. Owner decision.
5. **Launch in January if possible.** Education installs retain better then,
   and it is when people resolve to learn. Earlier is fine; January is the
   push.
6. **Native voices are a soft spot.** uTalk markets real speakers against
   "artificial voices". Recording a native speaker for the most-heard items
   (the 40 letters first) would remove the objection. Costed in the backlog.
